# Episode 1 — "The Pouch"

**Series:** Money Tails
**Core concept:** Pay yourself first / the emergency fund
**Supporting idea:** Buy Now Pay Later ties up future money
**Runtime target:** 6:00
**Australian references:** high-interest savings account, Afterpay, Moneysmart budget planner, rent increase

---

## Character bible (for consistent AI generation)

**KANGA** — Eastern grey kangaroo, early 30s energy. Warm, dry sense of humour, never lectures. Wears a light green short-sleeved t-shirt under a simple olive-green work vest. Her pouch glows faintly gold when money goes in. Voice: calm, Australian, slightly amused.

**MAGGIE** — Australian magpie, 20s. Fast-talking, loves anything shiny, charming and a bit chaotic. Wears mismatched bright sneakers and a sling bag full of gadgets. Voice: quick, upbeat, easily excited.

**HOPPER** — Green grasshopper, 20s. Laid-back musician, always has a tiny guitar. Lives in the moment. Wears a bucket hat. Voice: relaxed, slow, surfer-ish.

**Setting:** A sunny coastal Australian town. Weatherboard houses, a milk bar, gum trees, a beach in the background. Bright, saturated colour palette. 2D cartoon style, thick clean outlines, flat shading.

---

## Script

### COLD OPEN (0:00–0:15)

**SHOT 1** — Wide, town street, golden morning light. MAGGIE swoops across frame, eyes locked on a sparkling phone case in a shop window. HOPPER reclines on a bench strumming his guitar.

**KANGA (V.O.):**
Two mates. One payday. And a week that's about to go very differently for each of them.

**SHOT 2** — Title card: "THE POUCH". Gold coins drop into a kangaroo pouch silhouette. Quick, punchy sound.

---

### ACT ONE — PAYDAY (0:15–1:15)

**SHOT 3** — Split screen, three phones buzzing with the same notification: "Pay received."

**MAGGIE:**
Payday! Oh, I have been WAITING for this.

**HOPPER:**
Sweet. Takeaway tonight. Gig on Saturday. Sorted.

**KANGA:**
Nice. Gimme one sec.

**SHOT 4** — Close on Kanga's phone. She taps once. Animation: a chunk of her pay (labelled "10%") slides from "Everyday Account" into a second account labelled "Savings — The Pouch". Her actual pouch glows gold.

**MAGGIE:**
Did you just... pay someone before you've even had breakfast?

**KANGA:**
Yep. Paid myself.

**HOPPER:**
That's not how money works, Kanga.

**KANGA:**
*(grinning)* It's exactly how it works. Go on, do your thing.

**SHOT 5** — Montage, upbeat music. Maggie at the shop counter, tapping "Afterpay — 4 payments of $80". The phone case, a pair of sunnies, a speaker. Hopper ordering food, buying gig tickets, shouting a round. Kanga buys a coffee and a loaf of bread, walks home.

**SHOT 6** — Three side-by-side bank balances at end of day:
- Maggie: $210 (with "Afterpay owing: $960" in small red text underneath)
- Hopper: $40
- Kanga: $580 in Everyday, $2,400 in The Pouch

---

### ACT TWO — THE HIT (1:15–2:30)

**SHOT 7** — Tuesday. Grey clouds roll in. Three letterboxes. Three identical envelopes.

**LANDLORD (V.O., dull and official):**
Please be advised your rent will increase by $40 per week effective from next month.

**SHOT 8** — Same afternoon. Hopper's little scooter coughs smoke and dies on the road. Mechanic (a wombat in overalls) holds up a clipboard: "$640".

**HOPPER:**
Six hundred and... mate, I've got forty dollars and a guitar.

**SHOT 9** — Maggie at home, staring at her phone. Calendar shows Afterpay payments landing every Friday for the next two months.

**MAGGIE:**
I've got money! I just... don't have it *yet*. It's already spoken for.

**SHOT 10** — Kanga's place. Same envelope. She reads it, shrugs, pats her pouch. The pouch glows. Cut to her phone: a transfer from The Pouch covering the gap. No stress.

**KANGA:**
*(to herself)* That's what you're for, mate.

**SHOT 11** — Evening. Hopper and Maggie on Kanga's verandah, slumped.

**HOPPER:**
How are you not freaking out?

**KANGA:**
Because the freak-out already happened. Three years ago. And then I built a pouch.

---

### ACT THREE — THE LESSON (2:30–4:30)

**SHOT 12** — Kanga stands, draws on a whiteboard (or the scene shifts to a clean animated "explainer space" with a chalk-style background).

**KANGA:**
Most people pay everyone else first. Rent, phone, food, fun. Then whatever's left, they save. And whatever's left is usually nothing.

**SHOT 13** — Animation: a pay packet gets chopped into slices labelled Rent, Food, Fun, Subscriptions. A tiny crumb labelled "Savings" falls off the end and rolls away.

**KANGA:**
Flip it. Pay yourself first. Savings becomes a bill you owe yourself, and it comes out the second the money lands. Before you even see it.

**SHOT 14** — Animation: same pay packet. First slice comes off labelled "Me — 10%", goes straight into a pouch. The rest gets divided as before.

**MAGGIE:**
But I'd miss it.

**KANGA:**
You'd miss it for about two weeks. Then you forget it was ever there. That's the whole trick. It's automatic, so you never have to be disciplined. Your bank's doing the discipline for you.

**SHOT 15** — Phone screen close-up, generic bank app. Kanga taps "Set up automatic transfer". Fields: Amount, Frequency = "Every payday", To = "Savings".

**KANGA:**
Two minutes to set up. Put it in a separate high-interest savings account so it earns a bit while it sits there and it's not staring at you every time you open the app.

**HOPPER:**
How much do I need in it?

**SHOT 16** — Animation: a pouch filling up with three labelled segments.

**KANGA:**
Step one, a thousand dollars. That's enough to handle a dead scooter or a dentist without panicking.

Step two, keep going until it's about three months of your basic expenses. Rent, food, bills. If you lost your job tomorrow, you'd have time to breathe.

**SHOT 17** — Quick cutaway. Maggie's Afterpay calendar fades over her face.

**KANGA:**
And Maggie, Buy Now Pay Later isn't free money. It's your future paydays, already spent. Every one of those Friday payments is a slice that can't go in the pouch.

**MAGGIE:**
*(wincing)* So I've got a pouch. It's just got a hole in it.

**KANGA:**
*(laughs)* Pretty much.

**SHOT 18** — Kanga's phone shows the Moneysmart budget planner (stylised, not a screenshot).

**KANGA:**
If you don't know your monthly expenses, there's a free budget planner on the government's Moneysmart site. Takes ten minutes, tells you your number.

---

### ACT FOUR — THE TURN (4:30–5:30)

**SHOT 19** — Next morning. Hopper, tongue out in concentration, sets up a transfer on his phone: "$50 — every payday — Savings". Hits confirm. Small fanfare.

**HOPPER:**
A thousand bucks. That's the goal. Then I can play whatever gig I want knowing the scooter's covered.

**SHOT 20** — Maggie at the shop window. A glittering new set of headphones. Her eyes go wide. Her wing hovers over the Afterpay button.

**MAGGIE:**
*(to herself)* Pouch first. Then the shiny thing.

She closes the shop app. Opens her bank app. Sets up "$60 — every payday — The Pouch". Looks at the headphones one more time.

**MAGGIE:**
I'm coming back for you. Just not on credit.

**SHOT 21** — Kanga watching from across the street, sips her coffee, nods.

---

### PAYOFF + TAG (5:30–6:00)

**SHOT 22** — Caption: "THREE MONTHS LATER". Sunny again. Hopper's scooter dies again (running gag). He laughs, pats his phone, pays the wombat mechanic on the spot.

**SHOT 23** — Maggie walks out of the shop wearing the headphones, bag in hand. No Afterpay notification. Just a normal receipt.

**MAGGIE:**
Paid in full, baby.

**SHOT 24** — All three on the verandah. Kanga's pouch glows. So does a small glow on Hopper's guitar case and Maggie's sling bag — their own "pouches".

**KANGA:**
Pay yourself first. Everything else works out around it.

**SHOT 25** — End card, clean background. Three lines appear one at a time with a soft chime:

1. **The rule:** Pay yourself first. Savings is a bill you owe you.
2. **Do it today:** Set up an automatic transfer to a separate savings account for every payday.
3. **The number:** $1,000 to start. Then three months of expenses.

**SHOT 26** — Teaser. Maggie holding a credit card that's grown fangs.

**KANGA (V.O.):**
Next time: Maggie gets a credit card. It goes about as well as you'd think.

**END.**

## Production notes

- **Voice casting:** Kanga should never sound smug. Her lines work because she's relaxed, not superior.
- **Recurring visual:** The glowing pouch is the series' brand. Every character who "gets it" earns their own glow.
- **Reusable asset:** The "pay packet sliced up" animation (Shots 13–14) can be reused in the budgeting and super episodes.
- **Numbers shown on screen** ($640 repair, $40 rent rise, $1,000 starter goal) are deliberately ordinary so adult viewers recognise them.
- **Layering check:** Kids follow the story and the pouch. Young adults get the BNPL and automation beats. Adults get the 3-month target and the high-interest saver tip.
- **Prompt hint for AI generation:** Describe each shot as "2D cartoon, flat shading, thick clean outlines, bright Australian coastal town, [character description], [action]". Keep character descriptions word-for-word identical across shots to limit drift.
